# Marks — open code, owned names

The RAPP ecosystem's answer to "who owns this" is deliberately split, and the
split is the strategy:

> **The code is open. The names are owned. Integration is the license.**

## The frame: land and buildings

Wildhaven Homes LLC is a homes company. It does real estate for humans **and**
for AIs — that is the whole point, and it is why the ecosystem's property model
reads like property:

```
   the free foundation          the built estate
   ───────────────────          ────────────────
   "RAPP" the stem              rapp-* / RAPP <thing>
   the RAPP Brainstem           Rappter / rappter-*
                                OpenRappter

   the land, and the            the digital real estate —
   front door                   where the AIs actually live

   never tolled                 owned, and worth owning
```

**You do not toll the land. You own the buildings.**

The kernel is always free. The front door is never tolled. That is a commitment,
not a phase — the Brainstem is the ecosystem's permanent first hit, and it stays
that way.

It is also why an enterprise strain can govern the Brainstem without forking it:
the ground floor is common land, and only the governance built on top carries a
mark.

## What is claimed

The **compound marks** — the `rapp-*` names and "RAPP&nbsp;&lt;thing&gt;" names —
are claimed by **Wildhaven Homes LLC** through use in commerce. `Rappter` and the `rappter-*` family are claimed the same way.

Every ecosystem repository carries an open-source code license. **None of those
licenses grant any right to the marks.**

## What is deliberately NOT claimed

Two exclusions, and they are load-bearing rather than oversights:

- **"RAPP" standing alone.** The stem is open. The claims are the compound
  marks built on it, not the syllable itself.
- **RAPP Brainstem / `rapp-brainstem`.** The Brainstem is the universal
  launchpad and stays unencumbered on purpose: anyone may run it, name it,
  teach it, and redistribute it. **The front door is never tolled.**

That second exclusion is why an enterprise strain
([rapp-light](https://github.com/kody-w/rapp-light)) can govern the Brainstem
without forking it — the thing being governed is free, and only the governance
product carries a mark. The engine stays everyone's.

## Integration is the license

**The first hit is free.** If your product genuinely integrates with the
ecosystem — runs the Brainstem, speaks a `rapp-*` protocol, publishes to the
RAR, rides the Train — you are licensed to use the marks to name and describe
that integration, with no permission slip.

Conditions:

1. The integration must actually exist and keep working.
2. The mark takes  with attribution to Wildhaven Homes LLC on first mention.
3. Your use must not claim to be the origin or the official version.

Revocable for abuse or confusion. Quality is what keeps the license free.

## Applying it to a repository

Every ecosystem repo carries:

- its open-source `LICENSE` (MIT across most of the estate; Apache-2.0 on
  `rapp-train`),
- a `TRADEMARK.md` stating the claim and deferring to the canonical record,
- a **License and marks** section in the README naming its own mark.

## The canonical record

The authoritative trademark record is
**[TRADEMARKS.md in kody-w/rapp-train](https://kody-w.github.io/rapp-train/TRADEMARKS.md)**.
Per-repo `TRADEMARK.md` files defer to it. Where any of them differ, the
canonical record governs — so the claim is corrected in exactly one place.

A per-repo file that over-claims is a real defect, not a harmless flourish:
asserting the open stem or the Brainstem would contradict the ecosystem's own
promise that the front door is never tolled.

## The name, kept straight

The legal entity is **Wildhaven Homes LLC**. That is the name that appears in
attribution, in `TRADEMARK.md`, and in every copyright line.

Where "AI homes" appears in the estate it is a **description of the business**,
not a second entity: Wildhaven builds homes for humans and for AIs. Use the
legal name for legal wording; use the description when describing what the
company does.
