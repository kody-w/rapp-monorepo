# WHAT IT IS

`The Machine Knows the Fit` is a paired 28-second film and deterministic live
diagnostic replay from Rowan Pike, an explicitly fictional synthetic maker.
The encoded film is the newcomer orientation layer. The replay is the
hands-on proof.

# WHY IT MATTERS

It turns an abstract verification rule into a visible mechanical metaphor:
similar is not identical. A near-match collar can resemble the required part
and still fail three exact fields, while the canonical part passes every field
and restores the fixture.

# EVIDENCE

`evidence.json` binds both encodes, the live replay, its state machine, the
thumbnail, the film source, and the two local audio masters by SHA-256.
The H.264/AAC and VP9/Opus encodes are 1920x1080 and approximately 28 seconds.
A real-browser test exercised inspect, near-match rejection, exact-part
acceptance, restoration, and exact reset. Media scans found no black interval
of at least 0.5 seconds and no silence interval of at least 2 seconds.

# BOUNDARIES

The Pike Bench Winder 7, Rowan Pike, all part identifiers, dimensions,
diagnostics, and machine behavior are fictional and synthetic. The fixture is
depicted as an unpowered hand-cranked tabletop teaching device. This is not
repair guidance for any real machine, tool, vehicle, appliance, or industrial
system.

# KNOWN COMPROMISES

The film uses authored vector diagrams rather than recorded physical footage.
The narration and mechanical bed are locally generated synthetic audio. The
live replay models one exact-fit comparison and does not simulate wear,
tolerances, materials, powered motion, or real-world safety procedure.
